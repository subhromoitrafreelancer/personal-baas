What you stop building

Most projects spend their first weeks on the same plumbing. Here is the plumbing, and what replaces it.

Schedule of work you skip
JobNormally you buildIn Stead
Data APIControllers and serializers per tableGenerated from your Postgres schema
Who sees whatChecks scattered across endpointsRow-level rules, in the database
Sign-inPasswords, sessions, resets, MFABuilt in, with TOTP MFA and lockout
FilesAn upload endpoint and storage wiringBuckets with per-user access
Server logicA service to deploy and keep aliveFunctions in an isolated worker
Scheduled workA cron hostA built-in scheduler
DocumentsA PDF pipelinePDFs generated on demand
SecretsEnv files passed aroundAn encrypted vault
Several appsA fresh stack per clientIsolated projects on one install

Know this before you install it

  • You run the server. That means backups and upgrades are yours. If nobody on your team wants that job, a managed service is the better call.
  • After you create a new project, PostgREST needs a restart to see its schema. It is one command.
  • It is early software, built by one person. I would like to hear what breaks.
  • Comfort with SQL helps a lot, because the database is where the rules live.
